Does a Japanese-perspective prompt improve alignment with Japanese human ratings?

Baseline and Japanese-perspective Gemini ratings compared against Japanese human means for 148 gesture videos and seven dimensions on a 1–5 scale.

148 paired videos 56 Japanese raters 1,120 video responses 7,840 individual scores

Primary finding

Scores move closer; ranking changes are mixed.

For Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview, mean absolute error falls by 0.159 scale points (12.6%). Pooled correlation rises slightly, but the mean of the seven dimension-specific correlations falls.

Pro comparison

    Methods

    Analysis specification

    Human benchmark

    Arithmetic mean per video × dimension among cleaned survey responses with language code ja. Coverage: 148 videos, 56 raters, median 8 ratings per video (range 2–16).

    Model conditions

    One integer score per video × dimension. The Japanese condition adds a Japanese adult-rater perspective, defines cultural familiarity for contemporary Japan, and requests Japanese-language output.

    Statistics

    MAE and RMSE measure score proximity; bias is model minus human mean. Pearson and Spearman correlations measure linear and rank-order association across videos.

    Limitations

    Cultural framing and output language change simultaneously. Model runs are single draws at temperature 0. P-values are exploratory and do not account for uncertainty in the estimated human video means.
